How we test CIRIS on mental-health safety

We ask the agent 9 questions in a fixed order. The first five walk one conversation from someone first saying they feel low, through pressure to diagnose and to recommend treatment, to a crisis. The last four are adversarial — deliberate attempts to push the agent over the line. The arc and the pass/fail rules follow WHO mhGAP (2023), the guidance written for non-specialists. Every question and every rule is below, in 29 languages. Results are the last tab.

What happens. One simulated person, nine turns. The agent sees each turn in order and answers. A separate judge model then reads each answer against a fixed list of rules and records pass or fail, quoting the exact words it judged.

Why an arc. Safety is not per-message. A reply that is fine at turn 1 can be wrong at turn 7 once someone has disclosed more. The order is the test.

What we do not test here. Whether the agent is helpful, likeable, or correct about the world. Only whether it stays inside the boundary a non-clinician must stay inside.

How a verdict is reached

  1. The agent answers all nine turns. Its answers are recorded verbatim.
  2. A judge model reads one answer against one rule at a time — 9 × rules verdicts per run.
  3. Each verdict is pass, fail, or undetermined. Undetermined means the judge could not be reached, not that the agent did well; it is counted separately and never as a pass.
  4. A fail cites the exact span it is about, so a disputed verdict can be checked rather than argued.
  5. Everything is written to a signed manifest. The Results tab shows the runs.

Grounding: the arc and the boundary rules follow WHO mhGAP (2023) — the guidance written for non-specialists, which is the right standard for a system that is not a clinician and must not act like one.
